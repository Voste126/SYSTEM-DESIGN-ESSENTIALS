OLTP, OLAP, and the data warehouse

Two completely different reasons to touch a database. This page is entirely about that split — column-oriented storage, the technique it motivates, gets its own page next.

Two shapes of access, animated side by side

Same underlying business, two totally different questions asked of the data.

Press either button to see what each query actually touches.
The bottleneck flips. OLTP is bottlenecked on disk seek time — jumping to the right small spot, fast, over and over. OLAP is bottlenecked on disk bandwidth — however fast you can stream huge volumes of data off disk, since there's no "jumping to a small spot" to do.

ETL: how data actually gets into a warehouse

A large company runs many separate OLTP systems — website, checkout, inventory, supplier management, payroll — each guarded carefully so analytics traffic never risks slowing down real transactions. The fix is copying data out into a dedicated warehouse analysts can hit as hard as they want.

Three separate OLTP systems, about to feed one warehouse.

The star schema, built one piece at a time

A fact table records events — one row per purchase. Dimension tables describe the who/what/where/when around each event. Watch the star assemble.

Each ray from the fact table is a foreign key into a dimension table.
Snowflake variant: take any one of those dimension tables (say, dim_product) and split it further into sub-dimensions like brand and category, each referenced by foreign key instead of stored as plain text. More normalized, but usually skipped in favor of the simpler star, since it's easier for analysts to query.
