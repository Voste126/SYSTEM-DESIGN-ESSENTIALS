Comparing B-trees and LSM-trees

Rule of thumb: LSM-trees win on writes, B-trees win on reads — but "write amplification" is the actual mechanism behind that rule, and it's worth seeing rather than just accepting.

Write amplification, animated

A B-tree writes every change at least twice — once to the write-ahead log, once to the actual page (sometimes twice, to guard against a partially-written page surviving a power failure). LSM-trees rewrite data too, just through repeated compaction. Press play to watch one logical write turn into several physical ones, for both.

Why this matters beyond disk space. Write amplification is especially costly on SSDs, which can only survive a limited number of block overwrites before wearing out — so it's a durability concern, not just a performance one.

The honest bottom line: B-trees are deeply entrenched and reliable across many workloads; LSM-trees are gaining ground, especially in newer datastores. There's no universal winner — this needs testing against your actual workload, not a rule of thumb applied blindly.
