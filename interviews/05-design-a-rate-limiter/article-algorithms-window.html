Fixed window, sliding log, sliding counter

Fixed window has a real, exploitable flaw at its edges. The other two exist purely to fix that one flaw, at two different price points.

Fixed window counter — and its edge-burst problem

Divide time into fixed windows, count requests per window, reset at the boundary. Simple — until traffic clusters right at the seam between two windows.

2:00:00–2:00:30
2:00:30–2:01:00
2:01:00–2:01:30
2:01:30–2:02:00
Limit: 5 requests per minute. Watch what a rolling minute spanning the boundary actually sees.
The problem, exactly: 5 requests land in the last instant of minute 1, and 5 more in the first instant of minute 2. Neither fixed window individually exceeded its quota of 5 — but any rolling one-minute span straddling that boundary sees all 10, double the intended limit.

Sliding window log — the accurate fix

Keep an actual log of timestamps. Purge anything older than the rolling window on every request, then check the log size against the limit.

Limit: 2 requests per rolling minute.
Pro: genuinely accurate — no rolling window ever sees more than the limit. Con: every timestamp, even from a rejected request, can linger in memory until it ages out.

Sliding window counter — the memory-efficient compromise

Estimate the rolling count instead of logging every timestamp: current window's count, plus the previous window's count weighted by how much it still overlaps the rolling window.

Limit: 7/minute. Previous window: 5 requests. Current window so far: 3, at the 30% mark.
The formula in one line: current window count + (previous window count × overlap fraction) = 3 + (5 × 0.7) = 6.5, rounded down to 6 — under the limit of 7, so this request goes through. Trade-off: it's an approximation (assumes even distribution within the previous window) — but real-world measurement found this mis-classifies only a tiny fraction of a percent of requests in practice.
