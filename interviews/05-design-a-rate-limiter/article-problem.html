Design a rate limiter

A rate limiter caps how many requests a client can send in a window of time. Simple to state, genuinely hard to get right at scale — six pages, every subtopic on its own.

Why this exists

prevent abuse

Stops resource exhaustion from a denial-of-service attack, intentional or accidental — Twitter and Google Docs both publish hard per-user caps for exactly this reason.

control cost

When you pay per call for a third-party API (credit checks, payments, health records), an uncapped client can turn into an uncapped bill.

protect your own servers

Filters out excess load from bots or simply misbehaving clients before it reaches your real infrastructure.

Watch what happens without one

One client, no throttling in place.

The requirements, stated plainly

Accurate — enforce the actual limit, not an approximation that's wildly off.
Low latency — never meaningfully slow down a request that isn't over the limit.
Memory efficient — this runs on the hot path of every single request.
Distributed — must work correctly across many servers, not just one.
Clear feedback — a throttled client should get an unambiguous signal, not a mystery failure.
Fault tolerant — if the limiter's own infrastructure fails, it shouldn't take the rest of the system down with it.
