Where should the rate limiter live?

Client-side, server-side, or a dedicated middleware — one of these three is clearly wrong, and the other two are a real design tradeoff.

Three places it could go

client-side

The wrong choice in almost every case — client requests are trivially forgeable, and you frequently don't even control the client's code at all.

inside each API server

Works, but couples throttling logic directly into your application code, and every server needs to agree on shared state.

a dedicated middleware / API gateway

A layer in front of your real servers that inspects and throttles requests before they ever arrive — the most common real-world pattern.

A limit of 2 requests/second, about to receive 3.

Deciding: build it yourself, or use a gateway?

Lean toward building it yourself when: your current stack is efficient for this kind of work, you need full control over the specific algorithm your business needs, or you already have the engineering bandwidth to build and maintain it.
Lean toward a managed API gateway when: you already run one for other cross-cutting concerns (auth, IP allow-listing, SSL termination) and adding rate limiting there is the path of least resistance, or you simply don't have the engineering time to build and maintain a custom service.

There's no universally correct answer here — it depends on your team's current stack, priorities, and available engineering time, not on some objectively "better" architecture.
