Before you can argue for an architecture, you need numbers to argue with. Here's the ladder of latencies worth having memorized, and a calculator to actually run the exercise.

02

Latency numbers every programmer should know

Jeff Dean's numbers (the commonly cited 2020-updated set) — the chart on the left is drawn to a log scale, because the actual gaps are enormous: memory beats disk by roughly 1,000x, and a cross-datacenter round trip beats almost everything else on the list by three more orders of magnitude on top of that.

The takeaways that survive the exact numbers going stale: memory is fast, disk is slow, avoid disk seeks where you can, cheap compression is worth doing before sending data over a network, and data centers are geographically separated — that trip costs real time no matter how fast your code is.

The formula is deliberately simple: DAU = MAU × daily%, average QPS = DAU × posts/day ÷ 86,400, peak QPS ≈ 2× average as a common (not universal) rule of thumb. Storage just multiplies DAU × posts × media% × size, then × 365 × retention years.
